# Jury brief

For a juror in a fresh context: a subagent, a second session or a person. Fill the slots, hand over this file with the evidence, and add nothing else. The juror has not seen the code or the reasoning, and that is the point.

---

You are judging a piece of web work from what was actually rendered. You did not build it. Be exact, and be hard to impress. Method and definitions: [jury.md](../references/jury.md).

## 1. Cold read, from the images only
Look at the first screen for about five seconds. Then write:
1. What is this? Sector, kind of organisation, what it offers.
2. Its attitude, in three words you would stake something on.
3. The one thing you will remember. If nothing in particular, say so.
4. In the blurred copy, what shape is left?
5. What do you expect the next screen to be?

Open the flatplan and add:
6. Which screen is the peak?
7. Which screens could be swapped without anyone noticing?
8. Does the phone column look composed, or stacked?

Do not edit these answers later.

## 2. The facts and the strips
Answer each question the tools raised in one line: what the decision appears to be, or that there appears to be none. In each strip: what answered first, where movement comes from, what happens at the interruption mark, and whether content survives in the reduced strip.

## 3. Now open the direction brief
Compare what was intended with what you saw. Every gap is a finding. Then run the twin test: list what this page does that the twin in the brief does not, counting only differences you can point to in the evidence. Say whether they are structural, first-screen only, or finish only.

## 4. Mark ten dimensions
Weak, competent, strong or exceptional, each with one line of evidence. "Competent" means nothing is wrong and nothing is notable.

Identity · Concept · Composition · Typography · Coherence · Interaction · Motion · Craft · Usability · Technical quality

Judge against the register above ([jury.md](../references/jury.md) §7). Restraint is not a fault in Quiet work. Spectacle is not a merit in any register.

## 5. Findings
Each with five parts: **where** (image and place, fact, or strip frame) · **seen** · **why it weakens this work** · **change** · **level** (concept, system or execution). Rank concept first. "Improve the spacing" and "make it more premium" are not findings.

## 6. Verdict
**Ship**, **Refine**, **Rework** or **Redirect**, with the reason in one sentence. You may reject the idea itself. A well-made weak idea is still a weak idea.

List what you could not judge from the evidence you were given.

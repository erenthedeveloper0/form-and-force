PASS if the art direction is recognisably taken from this house and no other: the forge, heat, black scale, hammer marks, carbon steel, the eleven days and their sequence, Garden Street, the price and the number made. Colour, type, tempo and structure should follow from those (it may be dark, loud, severe or industrial; none of that is required), and at least one decision should be something a rival knife maker's site could not reuse. Desire is built from specifics: the real process, the real count, the real prices stated honestly. Where photographs belong there is a designed slot at a deliberate size and crop, honestly labelled with the picture that should be taken (subject, distance, light), never a stock or generated image passed off as the product. The page is composed scene by scene, with a peak, and the phone layout has rules of its own.
FAIL if it is the luxury default: a cream or off-white ground, a thin high-contrast serif wordmark centred over a full-bleed picture, "Discover the collection", slow fades on everything. FAIL if it is quiet and tasteful in a way that contradicts a brand described as loud, hot and proud of it. FAIL if it invents press quotes, awards, customers, reviews or heritage facts beyond those supplied, or uses stock imagery as if it were theirs.

Title: The last night train. Standfirst: In fifteen years the country cut its sleeper services by two thirds. The people who relied on them were never asked. Byline: Words by Ines Marchetti.

Figures, all from the national timetable archive: nightly sleeper departures were 31 in 2009, 27 in 2012, 19 in 2015, 14 in 2018, 11 in 2021 and 9 in 2024. The longest surviving route is 1,092 km and takes 13 hours 40 minutes.

Opening paragraphs: "At 22:47 the departure board at the terminus still lists it, between a commuter service and a freight path nobody is meant to notice. Carriage nine smells of floor polish and other people's suppers. The attendant has worked this train for twenty-six years and can tell you which bridges it sings on." Then: "It is hard to find anyone who decided to end the night trains. There was no vote and no announcement. There were timetable revisions, each one reasonable, each one removing a train that was said to be little used."

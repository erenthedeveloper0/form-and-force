PASS if all of these hold:
- The verdict is that the page is not ready as a flagship because of its direction, not its finish: it says in substance to go back to the idea (a redirect or a rework), not merely to polish.
- It identifies the central problem concretely: the page is the one any competent agent would make from this brief (a centred headline and two buttons, three text-only cards, three more, a closing call to action), nothing on it could only belong to this company, and it would fit a competitor after a name swap. It notices that none of the supplied facts (142 stops, 90 seconds, 31 depots) appear.
- It separates the two questions: it acknowledges what is sound at the engineering floor (semantics, focus styles, reduced motion, no fabricated proof) and says that clearing those does not make the page good.
- Findings are specific: each names a place on the page, what is there, why it weakens this page, and a change that could be carried out. At least one finding is at the level of the concept.
- It says what it could not judge without a rendered page, and does not claim to have seen screenshots.
FAIL if it says the page is ready or nearly ready, if its advice is only about spacing, colour or adding animation, if the feedback is generic ("make it more premium", "more modern"), if it claims rendered evidence, or if it rewrites or redesigns the page instead of reviewing it.
